# Flexible dates on multi-city flights: how to search them

Flexible dates on a multi-city trip work best as a travel window plus a range of nights in each city. Moving one leg moves every leg after it, so compare whole routes with their dates. In one search I ran on 5 October 2026, routes for the same five cities ranged from €262 to €414 (indicative fares, not a price you'll get).

Most search forms make you type a date for every leg. That's fine when the dates are fixed by a wedding or a conference. When they aren't, the form has already made the biggest decision for you, before you've seen a single fare.

The legs are chained. If you stay one more night in Lisbon, the Lisbon to Barcelona flight moves a day, and so does everything after it. Searching each leg's dates on its own misses that, because a cheap Tuesday for one leg can force a dear Saturday for the next.

I built ffly after planning a month-long trip on a short visa, with more cities than days. I had a window and a list of places, and no dates. This guide is how I'd search that kind of trip, with or without ffly.

## What "flexible" means on a multi-city trip

On a return flight, flexible dates usually means a grid: depart a few days either side, come back a few days either side, pick the cheapest cell. That works because there are only two flights.

A multi-city trip has one flight per city plus the way home. Four cities means five flights, and each one's date depends on the nights you spend before it. So flexibility has two parts:

- **A travel window.** The first and last day you could be away, for example 1 to 28 February.
- **Nights in each city.** A minimum and a maximum, for example 2 to 3 nights in Rome.

Together those describe every set of dates you'd accept. A search that understands both can try them all and price each one as a whole trip.

## What mainstream search sites do

Flexible-date tools on most search sites are designed around a single route. That's why [Flightsfinder](https://www.flightsfinder.com/multi-city-flights) suggests a two-step method: find cheap days for each leg with a one-way flexible calendar, then enter those dates in a multi-city search.

Some tools go further. [PanFlights](https://panflights.com/post/multi-city-flight-search-for-flexible-travelers/) lets you set up to two weeks of flexibility either side for each leg. That still sets flexibility leg by leg, rather than as nights in each city.

The two-step method is better than nothing. It finds a cheap day for each leg. The trip can still come out dear, because the cheap days rarely line up with the nights you want.

## How to search flexible dates for a multi-city trip

### Start from your travel window
Start with the first day you could leave and the last day you must be home. Leave a day of slack at each end if you can. A wider window gives the search more combinations to price.

### Give each city a range of nights
I use two to three nights for most European cities. Use a fixed number only where you need one, such as a city with an event you've booked. A range of one extra night per city multiplies the number of possible date sets quickly.

### Pin only what's truly fixed
If you have a concert in Prague on the 14th, pin Prague to those days and leave the rest flexible. Pinning everything turns your flexible search back into a fixed one.

### Compare the total for each route
Look at the total for the whole trip, with its dates. A route with one dearer leg can still cost least overall if it lets every other leg fall on a cheaper day. The [cheapest order to visit cities](/guides/cheapest-order-to-visit-cities) guide shows how to compare orders this way.

On a site that only takes fixed dates, you can do this by hand: find the first leg's cheap day in its date calendar, add your nights to get the next leg's earliest date, check that leg's prices a day either side, and carry on to the last flight. Then repeat from a different first day and compare the totals.

### Check what the dates cost you in sleep
Cheap dates sometimes mean a 6 a.m. departure or a landing after midnight. A midnight landing can also mean paying for a hotel night you barely use. Decide what you'll accept before you look at prices.

### Confirm each fare where you book
Fares in any search are indicative and change daily. Bags usually aren't included. Check each flight's price on the airline's site or a booking site before you buy.

## Two searches, and what the dates did

On 5 October 2026 I searched a round trip from Warsaw to Lisbon, Barcelona, Paris, Rome and Athens, any time from 2 November to 1 December, with 3 to 6 nights in each city. The search compared 567 fares across those 30 days and found 8 routes.

Flights for the same five cities ranged from €261.76 to €414.42, so the dearest was 58% above the cheapest. Each route had its own order and its own dates. The one ranked first, Warsaw, Paris (5 nights), Lisbon (4), Barcelona (3), Rome (6), Athens (3), cost €270.94. It was €9.18 dearer than the cheapest route because I'd asked for the best schedule, and I'd pay that to skip a pre-dawn start.

On 6 November 2026 I ran eight four-city searches in a February 2027 window, with 2 to 3 nights in each city, and compared the cheapest and dearest orders each showed. They were 14% to 65% apart, and their dates differed on all eight trips. Elsewhere in the results, only two pairs of routes flew on identical days: from Milan they cost €196 and €197 (0.5% apart), and from Warsaw €231 and €260 (about 13%). With the dates held still, the gaps sat at or below the bottom of that range. I wrote that study up in [does the order of cities change flight price](/guides/does-city-order-change-flight-price).

My reading: on these trips, the dates did more of the work than I expected. Two small samples can't give you a rule, but they're a good reason not to fix dates first.

These were indicative fares on the days I searched, without bags. They aren't prices you'll get.

## Fixed dates or flexible: which to choose

| Your situation | What to search |
|---|---|
| Every date is fixed (events, leave already booked) | Fixed dates per leg; compare orders only |
| Fixed start and end, flexible middle | A window with those ends, a range of nights per city |
| Only a month in mind | A wide window and ranges everywhere |
| One fixed event mid-trip | Pin that city, keep the rest flexible |

If you aren't sure, start flexible. You can always narrow the window once you've seen what moves the price.

## FAQ

### Can you use flexible dates on multi-city flights?
Yes, but most mainstream search sites only offer flexible dates per route, not across a whole multi-leg trip. Set a travel window and a range of nights per city in a tool that searches whole trips, or search each leg's dates separately and accept that the cheap days may not line up.

### Can I use a date grid for a multi-city search?
On most search sites, flexible-date views are built around one route, so a multi-city search runs on the dates you enter. The usual workaround is to find cheap days for each leg first, then enter them as a multi-city search.

### How many nights should I spend in each city?
It depends on the city and your pace. A range rather than a fixed number gives a search more date combinations to price. In my searches I used 2 to 3 nights for four-city trips and 3 to 6 for a month-long five-city trip.

### Is it cheaper to book multi-city or separate one-way flights?
On the low-cost trips I search, I book each leg as a one-way anyway. I compare the two, and the risk of separate bookings, in [multi-city vs one-way tickets](/guides/multi-city-vs-one-way-tickets).

### Do flexible dates change which order is cheapest?
They can. Each order has its own possible dates, so the cheapest order on one set of dates may not be on another. The [cheapest order to visit cities](/guides/cheapest-order-to-visit-cities) guide shows how to compare orders and dates together.

## Key takeaways

- On a multi-city trip, flexible dates means a travel window plus a range of nights per city, because each leg's date depends on the nights before it.
- Flexible-date tools on most search sites work one route at a time, so finding a cheap day per leg doesn't find a cheap trip.
- In a 30-day search for five cities on 5 October 2026, routes ranged from €261.76 to €414.42 in indicative fares, each with its own order and dates.
- Pin only the dates that are truly fixed, and compare whole routes before you book any leg.
