## Top-2 article recommendations

### 1. `cheapest order to visit cities` (Cluster A pillar)

- **Intent:** how-to. The reader already has a list of cities and wants to know which sequence costs least.
- **Why it fits ffly truthfully:** ffly finds the order and dates where the whole trip costs least for a chosen set of cities, a date window and nights per city. That is the exact question.
- **SERP gap:** Google currently answers a different question ("cheapest cities to fly into"). The only order-focused pages are Kiwi Nomad posts with old screenshots and non-European examples. Itinerary blogs reason only from geography ("don't zig-zag"). No ranking page runs the same European city set in several orders and shows what changes.
- **Format that ranks:** a 1,500-2,000-word how-to with a direct answer in the first paragraph, H2 steps, one worked example, and an FAQ block. Lists and itinerary posts in this space run 1.5-3k words.
- **No cannibalisation:** the home page owns "cheap multi-city flights / multi-city trip planner" (tool intent). This page owns the order question (method intent) and links to the home page and to ffly.app/search.
- **Angle (first-person example):** "I gave ffly four cities [e.g. Riga, Milan, Barcelona, Lisbon], a two-week window and nights per city. Here is the order it picked, and why it was not the order I would have drawn on a map." Explain the mechanism: low-cost carriers price each leg one-way, so both the sequence and the day each leg flies change the total. With 4 cities there are 24 orders before dates even vary, which is why doing it by hand in Google Flights is slow (Google Flights takes your order as given, up to five legs). **Show which order won and which lost, and describe the difference without quoting fares.** Note that ffly also favours sensible flight times, so the cheapest result is not always a 6 a.m. departure. Close with honest caveats: each flight is a separate booking on the airline's site, so allow buffer time; ffly is flights only (no trains, no hotels).
- **FAQ candidates (People Also Ask style):**
  - Does the order you visit cities change the price of flights?
  - How do I find the cheapest route between multiple cities?
  - Is it cheaper to fly into one city and out of another? (open jaw)
  - Is it cheaper to book multi-city or separate one-way flights? (link to recommendation #2)
  - How many nights should I spend in each city?
  - How many possible orders are there for N cities? (the travelling-salesman intuition)

### 2. `multi-city vs one-way tickets` (Cluster B pillar), framed for European low-cost carriers

- **Intent:** comparison. "Should I book this trip as one multi-city ticket or as separate one-ways?"
- **Why it fits ffly truthfully:** ffly's results are separate low-cost flights that you book one by one on the airline's or a booking site. The honest answer for intra-Europe low-cost trips is that ticket type matters less than order and dates, which leads straight to ffly without overclaiming.
- **SERP gap:** ranking pages (Skyscanner advice, Going glossary, Dollar Flight Club, Thrifty Traveler) are written for long-haul legacy fares, where multi-city pricing can beat one-ways. Forum consensus (Tripadvisor, Rick Steves) says budget carriers price point to point, but nobody shows a concrete low-cost Europe example. Ryanair MultiCity offers no discount and is effectively two one-ways (Irish Travel Addict). WIZZ Link combines only Wizz flights, as a self-transfer. Nobody puts these facts in one place.
- **Format that ranks:** a 1,500-2,500-word comparison guide with a quick answer up top, a comparison table (multi-city ticket vs separate one-ways vs airline multi-city tools: what each protects you from, what each costs you in flexibility), pros and cons, and an FAQ. Open jaw is covered here as a short H3 that links to its own page, /guides/open-jaw-flights-europe (2026-10-09).
- **No cannibalisation:** this is a booking-mechanics query, not a tool query. It also stays distinct from #1: #1 is "which order", #2 is "which ticket type". Each links to the other.
- **Angle (first-person example):** "For a three-city low-cost trip I compared booking it through an airline's multi-city tool with booking the legs separately. Here is what changed (and what did not), and where the real saving came from: picking the order and days." Include a self-transfer section (missed connections on separate bookings are your own risk; WIZZ Link's ConnectSure is the one in-network exception found). Again, no quoted fares.
- **FAQ candidates (People Also Ask style):**
  - Is it cheaper to book multi-city or separate one-way flights?
  - Can you book multi-city flights on Ryanair?
  - Does Wizz Air have multi-city booking?
  - What is an open-jaw ticket?
  - What happens if I miss a flight on a separate ticket?
  - Is multi-city cheaper on budget airlines?

### Next in line

`Ryanair multi city` and `Wizz Air multi city` (weak SERPs with spam pages; both feed the B-pillar), then `traveling salesman problem flights` (unclaimed niche that feeds the A-pillar).
